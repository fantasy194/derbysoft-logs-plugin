# Business semantics and choosing a process

## Sources and scope

This guide derives from `docs/Perf Log V2 Standard.md`, `docs/Stream Log Standard(JSON).md`, the accompanying `Perf Log Process & Fields Standardization (1).xlsx`, the Java SDK and the project's viewer. The workbook has been extracted into linked per-sheet references with original sheet names, row numbers, all non-empty cells and special notes. [process-index.md](process-index.md) indexes its `process` rows. Some sheets are marked deprecated or “will removed”; do not present these as current without corroboration. Workbook rules may be service-specific, outdated or inconsistent; verify actual mappings and observed data.

The user clarified that PerfLog, despite its name, is often used to record business events. The companion internal tutorials are [Perf Log V2 Tutorial](https://derbysoft.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/STONE/pages/2878177453/Perf+Log+V2+Tutorial) (version 37, 2025-06-12) and [Stream Log V2 Tutorial](https://derbysoft.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/STONE/pages/1803354261/Stream+Log+V2+Tutorial) (version 43, 2023-12-28). Their retrieved text may omit image and macro content. Use the workbook and log standards for the field definitions below, and current service data for implementation behavior.

These are candidate processes, not an instruction to assume every step exists in a particular transaction. Select the exact sheet and rows from [process-index](process-index.md); open the linked sheet to see fields, constraints and notes.

## How to interpret and correlate

- PerfLog V2 changes dots in field names to underscores (`process.result` → `process_result`). The standard calls for UTC `timestamp`, `app_name`, `topic`, `region`, `host`, `process`, `process_result` (`Success`/`Fail`), and usually `process_duration` in milliseconds. Queue/size checks have exceptions; the standard says `CheckDataSize`, while the workbook's common sheet says `CheckSize`. Preserve that discrepancy and check actual records.
- `process_result` reports that process step. A failed availability process is specified to use `availability_result=NoAvail`, but `NoAvail` can also be a legitimate no-room result; do not infer `Fail` from `NoAvail`. Use `err_code`, `err_msg`, `err_source`, and supplier/channel error fields as available. An error source field is evidence of reported origin, not independent proof of root cause.
- `channel` and `supplier` are Derby codes, normally uppercase. A Derby, supplier, channel or Exchange reservation/hotel/room/rate-plan ID belongs to its own namespace. Compare corresponding fields only; shared names do not make IDs interchangeable. For reservations, `order_status` and `payment_status`, if present, are separate from `process_result`.
- `echo_token` identifies the request in PerfLog. As clarified for this plugin, use it as the exact candidate `trace_token` when searching StreamLog. StreamLog's standard describes `trace_token` as the request/response chain identifier for a transaction, normally the payload transaction ID where available; `transaction_id` is a payload token, not universally the same field. Do not silently promote `echo_token_channel`, `echo_token_derby` or `scenario_correlation_id` to the exact bridge.
- StreamLog `transaction_type` classifies the business (`Reservation`, `LiveCheck`, `ARI`, `HotelContent`, `Legacy`, `Payment`, `Marketing`, `Member`, `HotelProduct`, `TicketProduct` in the local standard). `stream_type` says `RQ`, `RS`, or `RQRS`. `source` and `destination` describe request roles (`DerbySoft`, `Supplier`, `Distributor`), not a reservation result. Stream `process` and `process_result` may be absent.
- StreamIndex metadata and the corresponding StreamLog payload can be separate. The viewer queries index entries by `client`, exact `trace_token`, and `@timestamp`, then retrieves the payload using `s3_file_path`, `offset`, and `length`; a StreamLog-only mode also exists. `message` may be `RAW` or `GZIP+BASE64` and can be truncated at recording time. Payload retrieval and decoding failures must remain visible.
- Log `timestamp` is UTC according to the standards; actual search indices may use `@timestamp` or a different mapping. Inspect the mapping and identify the time field before querying. Topic names like `perf_{client}_raw` and `streamindex_{client}_raw` identify logging streams, not guaranteed search-index names.
